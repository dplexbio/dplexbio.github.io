# Applications

Anywhere labs rely on real-time PCR today, edPCR offers a path to digital PCR precision without changing plates, thermocyclers, or throughput. These are some of the most promising applications.

## CLIA clinical laboratories

Clinical labs run large volumes of PCR every day and need results they can stand behind. Under current rules, validating laboratory-developed tests is the lab's responsibility under CAP and CLIA. An instrument that gives absolute quantification helps labs validate assays and pass independent review and certification. edPCR can enter the lab as a research-use-only (RUO) instrument alongside existing qPCR workflows, and it reads plates run on the thermocyclers the lab already owns.

## Urinary tract infections and E. coli testing

Urinary tract infections account for roughly 10 million clinical cases each year, and E. coli is the leading cause. Antibiotic susceptibility testing checks which antibiotics stop a pathogen from growing, and conventional methods can take about two days. edPCR is precise enough to detect bacterial growth much earlier, cutting required culture time about threefold. In a beta test at a CLIA laboratory, edPCR produced susceptibility results in about 4 hours. Faster answers mean patients can get the right antibiotic sooner.

## Infectious disease and pathogen genotyping

Digital PCR detects viral infections, such as SARS-CoV-2, that real-time PCR can miss. edPCR brings that sensitivity to high-volume testing, including viral load measurement. Multicolor detection also supports multiplex panels. One example is rapid chlamydia genotyping that can tell strains apart, including antibiotic-resistant strains.

## Research laboratories

For many research groups, digital PCR has been too expensive or too slow to use routinely. edPCR uses standard well-plate formats, low-cost reagents, and a workflow familiar to anyone who runs qPCR. Labs can apply digital PCR precision to everyday experiments such as copy-number measurement, rare-target detection, and assay validation. Because plates can run on a real-time PCR instrument, the same samples can give qPCR and digital PCR results side by side, which makes moving existing assays to digital PCR straightforward.

## Gene expression

Gene expression studies measure how much RNA a gene produces. Real-time PCR reports relative changes that depend on reference genes and standard curves. edPCR counts transcript copies directly, giving absolute numbers that are comparable across runs, instruments, and laboratories. Its wide dynamic range can measure both rare and abundant transcripts, and multicolor detection allows several targets in a single well.
